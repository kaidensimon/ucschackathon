Choose a look

Three looks for GX10 Council, each drawn as a study. They are studies, not finished screens: judge the mood, the colour and the lettering.

Every study shows the same four moments with the same words: one model speaking, all five speaking, seven seats just after a vote, and the released answer. Each has a Light / Dark switch and a button that plays one bubble fading in and out. Chat View is not shown, because it is black and white in every look. The question, files and answer are invented.

One thing that is the same in all three: a seat's colour belongs to its place at the table, so a model's colour differs between the five-seat and seven-seat panels. Within one real debate it never changes.

A. The Lit Table

A pale, quiet room with one dark wooden half-round table. A small warm lamp comes on in front of whichever model is speaking.

Open study A

Only you can judge

  • In the light theme the seats are dark leather colours round a dark table. Rich and calm, or heavy? Two lighter versions are inside the study: a little lighter and lighter still. Lighter seats are harder to tell apart for people with colour-vision loss.
  • A wooden table with a lamp per seat: warm and clear, or too much furniture?
  • Its text typeface draws zero with a slash, so 0 and O cannot be confused. Fine in an answer such as "$2,520", or distracting?

What a first-time viewer said (an agent shown only the pictures)

  • Strongest thing: the lit spot on the table. The eye finds the speaker at once, in light, dark and grey.
  • Calm and warm, but the dark table is heavy and pulls the eye.

B. Fair Copy

The debate is written down in front of you like minutes being taken, and the agreed answer arrives as the clean final copy. Agreement is counted in tally strokes.

Open study B

Only you can judge

  • The fine line patterns on the bodies. The study shows the first moment twice, with and without them. Without is calmer; with helps in grey and for colour-vision loss.
  • Heads are a pale tint with a coloured ring, not filled with the seat's colour as your PDF describes. Coloured enough?
  • Bubble text is slanted. A strip in the study shows it slanted and upright side by side: which reads better, especially in dark?

What a first-time viewer said

  • Strongest thing: the tally strokes as the vote meter, ruled off when the council agrees.
  • The calmest of the three, but thin and faint, and the patterns add fuss. Its bubbles are the hardest to read.

C. Cut-Out Council

Flat, one-colour cut-out figures with each model's name lettered large across its head. Everything else is plain ink on white, so the council is the only colour you see.

Open study C

Only you can judge

  • The figures have no eyes or mouth: the name is the face. Friendly enough to look at through a long debate, or too plain?
  • There is no accent colour at all. Every button and mark is dark ink; only the seven seats have colour. Calm, or bare?

What a first-time viewer said

  • Strongest thing: bold figures with the biggest, clearest names of the three. Strong in dark too.
  • Calm and clear, but closest to an ordinary office tool: the figures carry all the charm.

Three questions, answered in the chat

  1. Which do you want to live with: A, B, C, a blend (say which parts), or none?
  2. Does its dark version look as good as its light one?
  3. What one thing bothers you in it?

Both judges who compared the written plans put A first and split on second place. That is their view, not a decision: the pick is yours.
