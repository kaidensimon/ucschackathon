GX10 Council UI Plan
Oct 10, 2026 · @Lucas Ta
Goals
The app is a council of local AI models on the GX10 that debate your question and release
one agreed answer. They research only your own files, and the answer goes out when 80%
agree or the time limit runs out. Everything runs on the GX10, so nothing leaves your
network except model downloads.
A plain chat box: ask the council anything and get one answer back, with the vote and
the files it used.
Watch the debate your way: while the council works, a small switch at the bottom right
flips between Table View and Chat View.
Table View: the models sit around a half-circle table, with a one- or two-sentence
summary above whoever is talking that fades out after a few seconds.
Chat View: the full messages the models send each other, in black and white.
Learns carefully: a council.md file of lessons and your preferences, where every entry
has passed a council vote.
Calm and readable: warm low-contrast palette, generous spacing, smooth fades, light
and dark themes.
Design direction
The look is warm paper, soft clay accents and a friendly sans-serif interface with soft serif
titles, so it reads as calm and editorial rather than like a control panel.
Typography
Quiche Sans is a paid font byAdam Ladd, sold through Fontspring and Creative Market
and included with Adobe Fonts, so there are no open-source files for it. It is classed as a
display sans, which suits titles better than long chat text. The plan therefore uses an open
stack, all under the SIL Open Font License and free to self-host: a soft high-contrast serif
for titles, a warm humanist sans for everything you read, and a monospace for numbers.

Layout and navigation
The screen has two columns under a slim top bar. The sidebar holds New chat, History,
Library, Models and Settings, and the conversation fills the rest.
When the council starts working, the conversation opens a stage for the debate and a
small Table / Chat switch appears at the bottom right. When the council agrees, the stage
folds into a card above the answer, and clicking the card replays the debate. The top bar
shows the seated council and a status pill with the round, time left and agreement.
How a debate runs
A debate goes from your question to one agreed answer in five steps, and ends when 80%
of the seats agree or the time limit runs out.
1. Ask. Type your question and attach any files. The last council you used is seated by
default. The seat picker swaps in any 3 to 7 models or a saved council and sets the time
limit.
2. Spool up. The models load on the GX10. Each seat fades in at the table as its model
becomes ready, and the Table / Chat switch appears.

===== PAGE 5 =====
3. Research. Each agent searches your Library and the chat's attachments, reads the
passages it needs and cites them by file and page. There is no web search, so nothing
leaves your network.
4. Debate in rounds. Agents state a position, answer each other by name and revise. At
the end of each round, every agent states its current answer in one sentence, and the
engine checks which answers match.
5. Agree and release. Once 80% of seats back the same answer, one agent from that
group writes the final answer in one voice, citing your files. Agents who disagreed each
add a one-line note.
80% rounds up, so a council of three or four has to agree unanimously:
Time limit. You set it when you ask, with 5 minutes as the starting default and presets of 2,
5 and 10. If time runs out before 80% agree, the council releases the answer with the most
support, marked No consensus, with the vote split and each dissent.
Stepping in. The message box stays live during a debate, and what you type reaches the
council at the start of the next round. Pause holds the debate and the timer; Stop ends it
and releases the best answer so far.
The answer. It lands in the chat like any reply, headed with the vote, such as Agreed 4 of 5,
plus the rounds and time taken. It lists the files it cited and links to a replay of the debate.

===== PAGE 6 =====
The table. A half-circle with its flat side toward you; the agents sit along the curved
outer edge, spaced evenly for 3 to 7 seats. The middle shows the round, the time left
and an agreement meter, such as 3 of 5 agree, needs 4.
The agents. Each is a half-circle body under a full-circle head with the model's short
name inside, in its seat color. The agent who is talking gets a soft clay glow, and after
each vote a small check or cross sits on every head until the next round.
The bubbles. Above each agent, a one- or two-sentence summary of what it is saying,
updated as it thinks. A small fast model on the GX10 writes these lines from each
agent's text every few seconds.
No overlap. Each seat owns the space above it. Bubbles are no wider than the gap
between seats, the end seats push theirs outward, and a bubble that would touch a
neighbor's rises above it. Text stops at two lines.
Fading. A bubble fades in over 250 ms, stays about 8 seconds, then fades out slowly
over about 1.5 seconds. A new summary from the same agent crossfades in place, and
with reduced motion on, bubbles simply appear and disappear.
Clicking an agent opens a side sheet with everything it has said and cited in this
debate.

===== PAGE 7 =====
Chat View
Chat View shows the full messages the models send each other, in black and white, with
the same fonts as the rest of the app.
Messages. Each one shows the sender, who it is addressed to (all, or one model by
name), the time into the debate and any file citations as small outlined chips.
Rounds and votes. Each vote appears as a thin divider across the thread, such as
Round 1 vote · 2 of 5 agree.
Black and white only. Near-black text on white, or the reverse in dark mode, with grays
for names and times. Models are told apart by name, never by color.
Fits the screen. On a wide screen the thread splits into one column per model, and on
a narrow one it stays a single thread. A filter shows one model's messages, and the
view follows the newest message until you scroll up.
Models
The Models screen is where you download models and choose who sits at the table, with
a memory meter for the GX10.

===== PAGE 8 =====
Model library. Search Hugging Face for GGUF files, download with pause and resume,
and see each model's size. Downloading is the only time the app goes online.
Seat picker. Choose 3 to 7 models for a debate, or save a named council to reuse. The
same model can fill two seats with different roles when memory is tight.
Memory fit. Every seated model shares the GX10's 128 GB of unified memory, along
with the memory each one's context needs. The meter shows whether a council fits
before you start and suggests smaller versions or fewer seats when it does not. More
seats also share the GX10's processing power, so each turn takes longer.
Keeping them loaded. Each seated model runs in its own llama-server, so all of them
stay in memory for the whole debate. llama.cpp's newer router mode can manage
several models from one server, but published notes conflict on whether they all stay
loaded at once, so it needs a test on the GX10 first.
Learning: council.md
The council keeps a council.md file of lessons and your preferences. Nothing goes into it
until the council has debated the entry and 80% agree it is worth keeping. Unlike agents
that save whatever they notice, every entry has a reason, evidence and a vote behind it.

===== PAGE 9 =====
What it holds. Two sections: Lessons, which are mistakes not to repeat, and Your
preferences, which are how you like answers. Each entry is one rule with its scope (a
topic, a kind of question or one model), the evidence, the vote and the date.
Where candidates come from. Your corrections, failed checks such as a citation that
does not support its claim, and a dissent that later proves right. Preferences you state
or show more than once count too. Each answer has thumbs up and down, a This was
wrong box and a Remember this option; none writes to the file directly.
The review. After an answer is released, a clerk drafts each candidate as one rule with
its evidence. The council then debates four questions: is it true, will it matter again,
does it clash with an entry, and is it too broad? 80% must agree, the same bar as
answers.
Three outcomes. Accepted entries go into council.md with their evidence. A candidate
that needs more evidence waits until the same thing happens again. A rejected one is
logged with the reason and is not proposed again without new evidence.
Your explicit preferences. When you say to remember something, the council checks
the wording and any conflict with existing entries, but it cannot veto you.
Staying small. Every seat reads council.md at the start of each debate, so it has a
budget of about 2,000 tokens to start. When it fills, the council proposes merges and
retirements through the same review, starting with entries never used or later
contradicted.
You stay in charge. A Council file screen shows every entry with its evidence and vote,
plus the waiting and rejected lists. You can edit, pin or retire any entry, and every
change is versioned with its reason so it can be rolled back.
Seeing it work. In Chat View, an agent that applies an entry cites it as a chip, such as
council.md L7. In Table View, a review shows as a small card in the middle of the table
with its vote.

Chat and history
Chat. The conversation looks like any chat, with Markdown, code blocks and a message
box that sends on Enter. While the council works, a stage opens above the message box
and the Table / Chat switch appears at the bottom right. Each answer shows the vote, the
rounds, the time taken and the files it cited.
History. A searchable list of past chats, grouped by Today, Yesterday and Earlier, that you
can rename, pin, delete and export. Every debate is saved with each message, summary
and vote, so you can replay it later in Table View or Chat View. History lives on the GX10.
Documents and downloads
The council does its research only in your files, which you give it in two ways and which
never leave your network.
Attach to a chat. Drag files into the message box or use the paperclip. The first version
reads PDF, Word, plain text, Markdown, spreadsheets and code, with OCR for scans and
images. Each file shows as a chip with its page count, and every agent can search it during
the debate.

===== PAGE 11 =====
Library. A Library screen holds files you want the council to use again. Drag them in, save
an attachment to it, or let the small Mac app watch a folder such as Downloads. New files
from a watched folder wait in an inbox for one click, and the council searches the Library
only when a chat allows it.
How agents read files. Several models share the GX10's memory, so each has a smaller
context than one model alone would. Agents therefore search for the relevant passages
by default, and only short files go in whole. Every answer and every Chat View message
cites the file and page it drew on.

Open questions
Decided so far:
The council runs on the GX10 alone; the laptop cluster plan is dropped, and your Macs
only open the app.
3 to 7 seats per debate, chosen when you ask.
The council agrees when 80% of seats back the same answer, within a time limit.
Research uses only your files, attachments and the Library, never the web.
A council.md file learns lessons and your preferences, and an entry gets in only after a
council vote.
Table View and Chat View, switched by a small box at the bottom right while the

Just you using it, locally, so no accounts or login.
Still open:
If time runs out before 80% agree, should the council release its best-supported
answer marked No consensus, as this plan assumes, or keep going?
What should the default time limit be? The plan assumes 5 minutes.
Does "based on resolution" for Chat View mean it adapts to screen size, one column
per model on a wide screen and one thread on a narrow one?
